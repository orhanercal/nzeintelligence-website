Buildings use around a third of the world's energy

That demand drives emissions, exposes us to energy price shocks and keeps us dependent on imported fossil fuels. Yet most net zero claims only balance one year of operation and miss three things:

  • Embodied energy is ignored

    Thicker envelopes, extra insulation and multiple glazing add energy to make. A building can use less each year while its whole-life energy rises.

  • Tools arrive too late

    Structure, materials, form and renewables are decided at concept stage, but detailed energy and life-cycle assessments usually come after.

  • Early design is too complex

    Form, orientation, glazing, shading and insulation interact in millions of combinations. Conventional simulation is too slow to explore them while designing.

One balance across the building's whole life

  1. Minimise what it takes to build

    Upload a BIM material take-off (Revit, Archicad or other BIM tools). Embodied energy is calculated for every material, and structural systems and lower-energy materials are compared side by side.

  2. Minimise what it uses

    Heating, cooling, lighting and appliances are calculated, with the design strategies and technologies that cut demand most.

  3. Generate the rest

    Solar PV and, where suitable, wind are sized to cover the minimised energy, and the platform shows when the building repays its energy debt.

Traditional houses never repay their energy. RNZE houses do.

Over an assumed 50-year service life, each traditional case-study house uses about 3.5 million kWh with no on-site generation. The same house designed with RNZE uses about 0.65 to 0.72 million kWh, including the energy to make its materials, and its renewables generate about 0.72 million kWh: enough to repay the full balance.

Coming next · 2027 to 2028

AI-assisted passive design

We are building an AI layer that predicts energy performance from early design parameters in seconds, not hours. It evaluates the whole space of passive design options, such as form, orientation, glazing, shading and insulation, and shows architects the most efficient combinations while the design is still open. AI supports the architect's judgement; it does not replace it.

The current prototype explores a first set of passive design parameters. The AI layer will add more, and a surrogate model will return results in about ten seconds, highlighting the options that best balance energy, daylight and comfort.

Our mission

Energy independence cannot be delivered by government investment alone. It requires every building, and everyone who designs, builds and lives in one, to become part of the solution. NZE Intelligence exists to make that possible from the very first design decision: less energy demand, lower-energy materials and clean energy generated where it is used.

Built on peer-reviewed research

NZE Intelligence turns the Realistic Net Zero Energy Building (RNZEB) method into a design tool. The method was developed in a Master's dissertation that scored 93.5 and won Brunel University London's Dean's Prize for Innovation and Impact, awarded to a single student each year across five engineering and management programmes.
